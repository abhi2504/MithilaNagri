**Durga Puja** is the great autumn festival of the goddess across Mithila — ten nights of *Navratri* worship of **Durga** as the slayer of the buffalo-demon, rising to **Vijayadashami**, the day of victory. In the Maithil towns of Darbhanga, Madhubani and beyond, *pandals* and old family temples house the clay image of the ten-armed goddess; the nights ring with the lamp-dance of **Jhijhiya** and the beat of the *dhak*.

Across the open border in the Madhesh and the hills of Nepal, the same festival is **Dashain** — the longest holiday of the Nepali year, when families gather, elders place the red *tika* and barley shoots on the young, and the goddess is honoured in every home. It is one of the clearest threads binding the two halves of the Mithila world, and a reminder that the region's calendar flows seamlessly across the frontier.
