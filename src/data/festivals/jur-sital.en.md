**Jur Sital** is the **Maithil New Year**, fixed by the solar calendar at **Mesha Sankranti** — the sun's entry into Aries, around **14 April**. Its name means "cool and at peace", and that idea runs through everything the day does. The **journey above** follows it from the eve of Satuani to the new-year greetings; here is what it means.

## A new year of coolness
Unlike the deity-festivals of the calendar, Jur Sital is a **seasonal and agrarian** turning-point: the close of the rabi harvest and the threshold of summer. Its eve, **Satuani**, is a harvest observance — people eat **sattu** (roasted gram flour), cooling and nourishing for the heat to come. On Jur Sital itself the hearth is rested and cold **basi** (leftover) food is eaten, embodying the "sital" spirit.

## The cooling water — and the earth
The festival's signature is a blessing of **coolness**: **elders sprinkle cool water on the heads of the young** for a calm year, while the young pour water over the elders' feet in respect. The cooling reaches the land too — people water trees and even roads to keep them green, **clean ponds, wells and drains**, and plant fresh saplings. This ecological renewal at the hottest turn of the year is a distinctive, and increasingly cherished, heart of the festival.

## Mithila Diwas
As the Maithil New Year's Day, people wear new clothes, visit kin and exchange wishes for good harvests. Since **2011 the Bihar government has observed the day as a statewide holiday, "Mithila Diwas,"** giving formal recognition to a festival that is, at heart, Mithila's gentle, green and intergenerational way of beginning the year.
