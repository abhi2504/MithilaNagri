**Sita Navami** (*Janaki Navami*) marks the appearance of **Sita** — daughter of Mithila — on Vaishakha Shukla Navami, a lunar month after Ram Navami. Nowhere is it dearer than in her own land. The **journey above** follows the legend from Janaka's plough to the fairs at her birth-sites; here is its meaning.

## Daughter of the furrow
By the Ramayana, a long famine drove King **Janaka** to plough the earth himself in a great rite to bring back the rains; the share struck a pot from which an infant girl rose in the **furrow** — *sita*, "furrow" — and he raised her as **Janaki**. She is thus revered as **Bhumija**, daughter of Mother Earth. (This is epic, devotional account, not dated history.) For Maithils she is not a distant goddess but the **literal daughter of the soil** — which is why this day sits at the very centre of the region's identity.

## How it is kept, and where
Devotees fast and worship Sita (often with Rama), read the Ramayana and honour the Earth Goddess; married couples pray for harmony. The day's great gatherings are at **Punaura Dham near Sitamarhi** (Bihar) and **Janaki Mandir at Janakpur** (Nepal). Their rival claims to be Sita's birthplace are old and unresolved — both are deeply venerated, and are now promoted together as a cross-border **"sacred Sita triangle"** (Sitamarhi–Punaura Dham–Janakpur). In 2025 the Government of India approved a ~₹883-crore redevelopment of Punaura Dham, modelled on Ayodhya, lifting the festival's profile.

## Distinct from Ram Navami
Though both fall on a Navami and both honour the house of Raghu, Sita Navami is its own day, a lunar month after Ram Navami — and in Mithila, the more heartfelt of the two: this is, above all, the **land of Sita**.
