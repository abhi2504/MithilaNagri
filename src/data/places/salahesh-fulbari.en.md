About 4 km west of Lahan in Siraha district, the **Salahesh Fulbari** is a roughly 14-acre sacred garden devoted to **Raja Salhesh**, the legendary king venerated as the chief deity and folk-hero of the **Dusadh (Dalit) community** across Mithila in both Nepal and Bihar. Unlike Mithila's Brahmanical temples, this is a Dalit pilgrimage centre — a rare and important counterpoint in the region's sacred landscape, and the central subject of *Salhesh*/Godna folk painting.

The grove's marvel is a **haram-tree orchid** held to bloom on the eve and first day of **Baishakh** (the Nepali New Year), revered as a living token of love between King Salhesh and his beloved Malini. Devotees regard the flowering as miraculous rather than merely botanical.

Each New Year a great **Fulbari Mela** draws thousands of pilgrims from Nepal and India, while related Salhesh shrines on the Bihar side keep his memory alive as a symbol of dignity and social equality.
