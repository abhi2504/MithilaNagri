In the village of **Saurath**, a few kilometres west of Madhubani town, lies the **Sabha Gachhi** — a 22-acre mango orchard said to have been gifted by the Maharaja of Darbhanga. For generations, during the months of **Jyestha–Asadh**, thousands of Maithil Brahmin families gathered here in the open air to arrange marriages, in what outsiders have called a "groom market."

The institution grows out of the **Panji** genealogical system formalised in the 14th century, with the registration of Mithila Brahmins beginning around **1326 CE**. At the heart of the sabha sit the *Panjikars* — registrars who consult the lineage records and issue an *asvajanapatra* certifying that prospective bride and groom share no near blood relationship, a safeguard against consanguinity. Fourteen villages once held such sabhas; today the tradition survives chiefly at Saurath. The campus also holds the **Madhaweshwar Nath Mahadev** temple.

Though attendance has dwindled from the vast crowds of a generation ago, the gachhi remains a living monument to Mithila's distinctive social engineering — and efforts continue to revive it as cultural heritage.
