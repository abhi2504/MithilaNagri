At **Kandaha**, a village in the Mahishi block of Saharsa, stands one of eastern Mithila's rare surviving Sun temples — an Archaeological Survey of India–protected shrine to **Surya (Suryanarayana)**. Its sanctum holds a black-stone image of the Sun god riding a seven-horse chariot, flanked by his consorts Sanjna and Chhaya, a sculptural type that links it to the wider tradition of Surya worship along the rivers of north Bihar.

A copper-plate inscription is read as dating the temple to **1357 CE** (some accounts give **1435 CE**); it is attributed to a **King Narasimha Deva**, with the founding dynasty given variously as **Karnat** or **Oiniwar**. Local tradition pushes its origins much further back, crediting Samba, son of Krishna, and counting it among twelve Suryanarayana shrines. Damaged in later centuries, it was renovated by the saint-poet Lakshminath Gosain, and is sometimes called the *Markandeyark* temple.

Because the temple sits in the Chhath heartland of the Kosi region, it draws Surya devotees especially around **Chhath** and in the month of Magh, when the Sun is most venerated in Mithila.
