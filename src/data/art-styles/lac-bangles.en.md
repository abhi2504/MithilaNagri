**Lac bangles** are made from *lac*, the natural resin secreted by the lac insect, and in Mithila and wider north Bihar they are the hereditary work of the **Laheri (Lakhera)** caste. The bangle is built in two layers: a cheap core of refuse lac bulked with white clay, sheathed in a thin skin of fine, brightly pigmented lac. Worked warm, the resin is kneaded into a dough, rolled and pressed over wooden formers, then decorated with mirror chips, glass beads and gold foil, or swirled into the cloud-like marbled finish called *abrikam*.

The craft carries deep ritual weight as an emblem of *suhag* (marital good fortune): coloured lac bangles are part of a Maithil bride's adornment, and the same artisans turn out lac **sindurdans** (vermilion containers) and small caskets given at weddings, objects tied to fertility and a long married life. **Sitamarhi** — especially **Sursand** — and **Muzaffarpur** are historic bangle centres, while **Madhubani** clusters are noted for lac bangles hand-painted with Mithila folk designs.

Today the trade survives on wedding-season and tourist demand but is squeezed by cheap glass, plastic and machine-made bangles, and many Laheri families have left the work. *(Lac bangle-making is a craft of greater Bihar generally; what is distinctly Maithil is the Laheri community's concentration in the Sitamarhi–Muzaffarpur–Madhubani belt and the bangles' place in Maithil bridal custom.)*
