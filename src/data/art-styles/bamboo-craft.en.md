**Bamboo craft** supplies the indispensable ritual vessels of Maithil life. Split bamboo is dressed into fine fibres and woven into the **daura** (a large carrying basket), the **soop** (a three-sided winnowing fan), the lidded **changera** ceremonial tray, and smaller forms like the **konia** and **dala**. In Mithila this weaving is largely the ancestral occupation of the **Dom** community, for whom the daura made for weddings has long been a livelihood.

These are not merely utilitarian objects but ritually charged ones. At a Maithil wedding the bride scatters parched rice (*lava*) into the sacred fire from a soop while her brother refills it; baskets of turmeric, vermilion, betel and sweets travel between the two families' homes in changeras; and at **Chhath** the soop and daura heaped with fruit and *thekua* are carried to the riverbank to offer to the sun. The konia and daura recur through *vat-savitri*, *madhushravani* and almost every domestic rite.

Made from a cheap, fast-renewing material and still woven by hand in village courtyards, the craft endures because the rituals that need it endure — but the weavers themselves remain among the poorest of artisans, squeezed by the rising cost of raw bamboo and competition from plastic and steel.
