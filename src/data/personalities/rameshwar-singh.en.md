**Maharaja Sir Rameshwar Singh** (1860–1929) ruled the vast **Darbhanga Raj** — the greatest zamindari estate of Mithila — from 1898 until his death, succeeding his brother Lakshmeshwar Singh. Unusually for a prince of his rank, he entered the **Indian Civil Service** in 1878 and served as a magistrate at Darbhanga, Chhapra and Bhagalpur; he later sat on the Viceroy's councils, becoming one of the most influential Indian voices in the colonial administration.

A devout **tantric and Srividya adept** regarded by his people as a *rajarshi* (sage-king), he convened a landmark conference of tantra scholars in 1908 attended by **Sir John Woodroffe (Arthur Avalon)**. His philanthropy was enormous: he was a co-founder and major early donor of **Banaras Hindu University** alongside Madan Mohan Malaviya, and gave generously to several other Indian universities.

Rameshwar Singh personifies the **modernising yet deeply traditional Maithil aristocracy** of the colonial era — a ruler who fused Sanskrit and tantric learning with civil administration and large-scale institution-building, leaving an imprint on Mithila's temples, schools and public life that endures.
