**Santosh Kumar Das** (born 1962) is among the most important living **Mithila (Madhubani) artists** and one of the few men to win renown in a tradition long carried by women. Born in the village of **Ranti**, just east of Madhubani, he learned the restrained, line-driven **Kachni** style from the women of his home — including the great painter **Mahasundari Devi** — before formally studying at the **Faculty of Fine Arts, M.S. University, Baroda**.

Where the classical Mithila idiom depicted gods and weddings, Das expanded its vocabulary toward the **contemporary and the political**: acclaimed series on Krishna, the Buddha and Yoga, and searing work such as his **Gujarat series** responding to the 2002 violence. From 2003 he helped found and teach at the **Mithila Art Institute** in Madhubani, training a new generation; his work is held in collections from Oberlin to Denver.

By marrying village ritual painting with the concerns of modern fine art, Santosh Kumar Das has shown that **Mithila painting is a living, evolving language** — capable of memory, protest and personal vision — and has secured its place in the contemporary art world.
