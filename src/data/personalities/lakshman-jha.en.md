**Lakshman Jha** (1916–2000), born at **Rasiari** in Darbhanga district, was the principal architect of the modern **Mithila statehood movement**. A nationalist from boyhood — he demonstrated against the Simon Commission and joined the Civil Disobedience and Quit India movements, serving on the Bihar committee during the 1942 uprising — he then turned his energy to the cause of Mithila and the Maithili language.

A gold-medallist in Sanskrit from Patna College, he earned a **PhD at the School of Oriental and African Studies (SOAS), University of London**, with a thesis on *Mithila and Magadha*. From **1952** he gave the demand for a separate **Mithila state** its modern organisational form, campaigning for it through the era of India's states' reorganisation.

Lakshman Jha stands as the intellectual and political founder of **Maithili sub-nationalism** — the figure who gave Mithila's distinct linguistic and cultural identity its modern organisational form as a claim for political recognition, a demand that still animates the region today.
