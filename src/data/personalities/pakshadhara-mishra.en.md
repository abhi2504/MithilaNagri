**Pakshadhara Mishra** (also known as **Jayadeva**), who flourished in the fifteenth century, was the pre-eminent *Naiyayika* (logician) of his age and the head teacher of **Nyaya Shastra** in Mithila. A court scholar under King **Bhairava Singh** of the Oiniwar dynasty, he led a celebrated academy of logic — for generations the most prestigious centre for the study of **Navya-Nyaya**, the "New Logic" founded by Gangesha Upadhyaya.

His best-known work, the ***Aloka*** gloss, made him the towering representative of the **Mithila school** of Navya-Nyaya. Students travelled from across India to study under him; among them were **Vasudeva Sarvabhauma** and the famous **Raghunatha Shiromani** of Bengal, who would carry the tradition to Nabadwip and eventually rival Mithila's supremacy.

Pakshadhara stands for Mithila at the height of its renown as **India's school of logic** — a place to which scholars made pilgrimage to master the most rigorous philosophical method of the medieval world, and whose jealous rules (the legend that no manuscript of logic could leave Mithila) became part of the region's intellectual lore.
