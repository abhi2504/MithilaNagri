The arrival of a child in a Maithil home is sung into being. The **journey above** follows the welcome from the first birth-songs to the first grain; here is what gives those days their meaning.

## Sung into the world
**Sohar** are the **birth-songs** of the Maithil-Bhojpuri world — sweet, swinging folk melodies raised by the women of the household and neighbourhood to celebrate a newborn (traditionally, especially, a son) and bless the mother. They are an old women's repertoire, sung from the night of birth through the days of seclusion and again at later milestones, and they often set the new child's arrival against the births of **Rama and Krishna** — making every birth, for a few days, a sacred one. A conch is blown at the news, the *dholak* keeps time, and the songs carry the family's joy.

## Chhathi — the night destiny is written
The tender heart of the welcome is **Chhathi**, the sixth day. Maithils believe that on this night the goddess **Shashthi** (Chhathi Maiya), protector of children, silently enters the home to **write the child's destiny** — so a lamp is lit and a **pen and paper left beside the baby** for the goddess to use. Mother and child take their first formal bath, the house is purified, and the seclusion of birth begins to lift. Why the sixth day, and why a goddess of children? Because infant mortality once made these first days the most perilous of a life — and so the most carefully guarded by rite and song.

## The unfolding of a life
Chhathi opens a sequence that marks and protects a young life: the twelfth-day **Barahi**; the **Namkaran** (naming), by Maithil convention on the twelfth day for a son and the eleventh for a daughter; the **Annaprashan**, the first feeding of grain (usually kheer) around the sixth month; and later the first haircut, [Mundan](/en/rituals/mundan/). Each is a small festival of relief and hope — the community's way of saying that another child has safely crossed into the family.
