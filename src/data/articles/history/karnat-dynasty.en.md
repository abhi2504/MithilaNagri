The **Karnat (Karnata) dynasty** founded the medieval kingdom of Mithila and gave the region roughly two and a quarter centuries of unified rule, conventionally dated **1097–1324 CE**. Its founder, **Nanyadeva**, was a warrior whose family tradition traces to the Karnataka south (hence "Karnat"), and his accession is among the most precisely datable events in Mithila's history: an inscribed pillar records that "in the Shaka year 1019… king Nanyadeva took the land," corresponding to **10 July 1097 CE**. He established his seat at the great fortified city of **Simraungadh**, on what is now the India–Nepal border in Bara district, Madhesh.

The dynasty's principal rulers are usually listed as **Nanyadeva** (r. c. 1097–1147), **Gangadeva**, **Narasimhadeva**, **Ramasimhadeva**, **Shaktisimhadeva** and **Harisimhadeva** (to c. 1324). These regnal dates should be read as approximate and reconstructed — they vary across sources and are inferred from inscriptions, chronicles and later genealogies rather than from a continuous record. Nanyadeva himself was remarkably a **musicologist** as well as a king, author of a Sanskrit treatise on music (the *Bharatabhashya*) later cited by the 13th-century authority Sharngadeva — a philosopher-king in the old Janaka mould.

The Karnat era was a cultural high-water mark. It saw **Gangesha Upadhyaya** found the **Navya-Nyaya** ("new logic") school that would make Mithila the leading centre of Indian philosophy for centuries, and **Jyotirishwar Thakur** compose the *Varnaratnakara* (early 14th c.), often called the earliest surviving prose work in Maithili. Tradition holds that the last great king, **Harisimhadeva**, instituted the **Panji** system of genealogical record-keeping around 1326 — the institution that still defines Maithil social memory.

The kingdom fell to the Delhi Sultanate. In **1324 CE**, **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq**, returning from a Bengal campaign, attacked Mithila; Harisimhadeva, rather than make a stand, abandoned Simraungadh and fled north into the Himalayan foothills of present-day Nepal. (Sources agree on the 1324 invasion but differ on later detail: Harisimhadeva is generally said to have reached the Dolakha/Nepal hills and died around **1326**, and some of his line are linked to later Nepali ruling houses — a point where documented history shades into chronicle tradition.)

The Karnat legacy is the very framework of medieval Mithila: a unified kingdom, a fortified capital whose earthwork ruins and Tirhuta-script inscriptions still survive at Simraungadh, the flowering of Navya-Nyaya and early Maithili letters, and the Panji institution — foundations on which the succeeding Oiniwar and Darbhanga dynasties would build.
