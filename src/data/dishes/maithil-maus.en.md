**Maus** (mutton) is the celebrated meat of Mithila, and **khassi (goat) is the preferred kind**. What sets the Maithil style apart is its restraint: the meat is traditionally cooked **without onion or garlic**, shallow-fried first in *desi ghee* and then simmered slowly in a light, almost soupy gravy of **ginger, coriander, cumin and red chilli** in mustard oil. The result is a clean, aromatic curry rather than a heavy masala one, eaten with rice.

Beyond the table, meat carries ritual weight: goat is offered to goddesses such as Durga and Kali during the autumn pujas, and the cooked *maus* is then shared as a feast. At a Maithil ceremony the great experience is the *bhoj-bhaat* — dishes served on a banana leaf to rows of guests seated on the floor — where a well-made *maus* is the dish guests remember.
