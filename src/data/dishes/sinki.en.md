**Sinki** is a traditional **fermented and sun-dried radish** preparation rooted in the Terai and hill kitchens of Nepal, including the Madhesh belt of Mithila. The **radish taproot** (not the leaves, which become *gundruk*) is grated, packed tightly and left to ferment for weeks until it turns sour, then sun-dried into a storable form that survives the lean months.

In the kitchen, dried sinki is soaked and made into a simple, warming **sour soup** (*jhol*): the slivers are sautéed with tomato and chilli in mustard oil, seasoned with turmeric and salt, and simmered until tender, then ladled over rice. It is a frugal, probiotic-rich winter food prepared as a household craft, traditionally led by rural women and passed down through generations — a Madhesh contribution to the broader Maithil table. *(Shared across several Himalayan/Terai communities, so not exclusively Maithil.)*
