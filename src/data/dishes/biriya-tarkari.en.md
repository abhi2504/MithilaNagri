**Biriya** is a distinctly Maithil preparation built around **dried chickpea greens** — the tender tops of the gram plant, sun-dried and dusted with a little flour into small pods that store through the year. Cooked into *tarkari*, the pods are lightly fried, then simmered with potato in a gravy of **mustard paste, garlic and mustard oil** sharpened with turmeric and chilli, and eaten with hot rice. It embodies two hallmarks of the cuisine: the heavy use of mustard, and the Maithil love of *saag* (greens), without which, it is said, no meal feels complete.

Because the greens are preserved by drying, biriya turns a seasonal crop into year-round food — a thrifty village dish that has become a marker of Maithil home cooking. It is everyday fare rather than festive: the kind of plate that tastes of a Mithila kitchen rather than a celebration.
