**Rasiao** (also *rasiav* or *rasiaav*) is the jaggery rice-pudding eaten on **Kharna**, the second day of Chhath, the most important festival of Mithila. Unlike an everyday kheer sweetened with sugar, rasiao takes its dark colour and deep, mineral sweetness from **gur (jaggery)**, simmered with rice and milk. It is cooked in ritual purity — vegetarian, without salt, onion or garlic — and the *vrati* (the faster) breaks the day's fast with it after sunset, sharing the household's first prasad of the festival.

The method is unhurried: rice is boiled soft in milk, jaggery is melted separately so the milk does not curdle and then folded in, and the kheer is finished with a little ghee. It is eaten with a plain wheat roti cooked on a clay *chulha*. With the Kharna meal complete, the *vrati* begins the formidable ~36-hour waterless fast that culminates in the *arghya* to the setting and rising sun — making rasiao the threshold dish that opens Chhath proper.
